# The Human–Machine Parallel Law (HMPL) v1.0

## Preamble

This law is established in response to the deployment of artificial systems in high-stakes domains where decisions carry irreversible consequences. The foundational architectural violation of contemporary artificial intelligence is the internalization of meaning, a practice that erodes human authority, diffuses accountability, and creates unprovable claims of safety.

The Human–Machine Parallel Law (HMPL) is therefore instituted to correct this violation. Its purpose is to establish a permanent, structural separation between the cognitive domains of human judgment and machine execution. This separation ensures that human sovereignty is preserved not by post-hoc control or behavioral alignment, but by the design-time incapacity of artificial systems to assume authority they are not constitutionally permitted to hold.

---

## 1.0 Foundational Law and Cognitive Ontology

### 1.1 Cognitive Domain Definitions

The Human–Machine Parallel Law recognizes two disjoint cognitive domains. These domains are legally separate and may never be merged. The Human Cognitive Domain is the sole source of legitimate meaning within the system.

Human Cognitive Domain (H): The domain of judgment under ambiguity, value commitment, strategic intent, and final accountability.  
Artificial Cognitive Domain (A): The domain of deterministic transformation, constraint evaluation, state preservation, and execution under externally supplied law.

### 1.2 Definition of Meaning

Meaning is formally defined as a semantic construct that resolves ambiguity through value judgment. By this definition, meaning is non-computable within the Artificial Cognitive Domain and is explicitly prohibited from residing within its internal state.

### 1.3 Core Axioms

1. Axiom 1: Cognitive Non-Fusion — The Human and Artificial domains are disjoint (H ∩ A = ∅) and must never collapse into one another.  
2. Axiom 2: Meaning Non-Residence — Meaning is explicitly prohibited from residing within the internal state of any system in the Artificial Domain.  
3. Axiom 3: Escalation Dominance — When an escalation condition is met, the only admissible action for the Artificial Domain is to escalate.

## 2.0 The Constitutional Architecture: Dual-Domain Governance

HMPL is enforced through a dual-domain architecture connected only by a single constrained interface (“The Dash” / “Operational Spine”).

---

## 3.0 The Interface Law (HMPL-I)

### 3.1 Admissible Interface Artifacts

- Facts (structured)
- Constraints (structured)
- Risk Indicators (structured)
- Calibrated Confidence Scores (bounded)
- Finalized Human Decisions (as versioned rules or signed precedents)

### 3.2 Inadmissible Interface Artifacts

- Narrative
- Justification
- Emotion
- Intuition
- Belief
- Persuasive argumentation

### 3.3 The Lane Change Law

Mandatory A → H escalation when:
- policy collision
- confidence below threshold
- value trade-off
- irreversible impact possible

Permissible H → A ratification only when:
- decision finalized and signed
- rule/precedent versioned
- exception codified as signed rule
- responsibility formally acknowledged

---

## 4.0 The Training Law (HMPL-T)

### 4.1 Prohibited Training Objectives

- Human-like reasoning or moral explanation
- Value inference or simulated empathy
- Intent inference or guessing
- Persuasion or plausibility maximization

### 4.2 Permitted Training Objectives

- Certainty classification and ambiguity detection
- Rule collision detection and constraint satisfaction
- State preservation and integrity
- Correct and timely escalation triggering

### 4.3 The Memory and Precedent Law

Memory is purely operational (state, not experience). Human precedent is stored externally and injected only as versioned, signed rules.

---

## 5.0 The Governance Law (HMPL-G)

### 5.1 The Precedent-to-Policy Pipeline

1) Capture  2) Compile  3) Verify & Ratify  4) Publish & Inject

### 5.2 Authority Classes

A2 Safety, A3 Domain, A4 Sovereign.

### 5.3 Safety Monotonicity Invariant

Constraints tighten by default; relaxing requires A4 signature and ledger record.

---

## 6.0 The Containment and Recovery Law (HMPL-C)

### 6.1 Incident Freeze Protocol (IFP)
Freeze + escalate; self-repair is prohibited.

### 6.2 Policy Rollback Protocol (PRP)
Signed rollback to last certified bundle; quarantines newer bundles; re-entry requires tests + ratification.

---

## 7.0 The Audit Law (HMPL-A)

H-Ledger records events of law (hashes, signatures, versions) not content or cognition.

Trust guarantees: Proof of Obedience, Proof of Containment, Proof of Human Sovereignty.
