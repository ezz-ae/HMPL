HMPL treats high‑stakes AI like an automated pharmaceutical factory: the machine is exceptionally fast at mixing chemicals and executing recipes, but it has no constitutional permission to “understand” health, ethics, or legitimacy. Those decisions live only with licensed human authorities who author signed rules and safety thresholds outside the machine. The only connection is a constrained interface that transmits structured state and signed policy—never narrative justification. If the system detects ambiguity, policy collision, low confidence, or an irreversible risk signal, it must freeze all execution lanes and escalate; guessing is a constitutional violation. In short: meaning and authority stay in human hands, execution stays in machine hands, and every change in governing policy is provable by signatures and hashes.
