Analogy (Regulator-Friendly)

Think of HMPL as an automated pharmaceutical factory. The factory (Machine) is incredibly fast and precise at mixing chemicals, but it has no understanding of what health is or why a medicine is needed. Those meaningful decisions—the recipes and the safety thresholds—are developed by scientists (Humans) in a separate lab. The only connection between the lab and the factory is a digital keypad. The scientist enters a code (the rule), and the factory executes it. If the factory's sensors detect an unknown chemical or a pressure spike (ambiguity), it doesn't try to guess a solution; it immediately shuts down all valves (freeze) and flashes a red light (escalate) for the scientist to intervene. Meaning—the "why" and the "should"—never enters the factory's pipes.
